# Schedule

Two days, 8:30 to 17:30, with a 60-minute lunch and two 15-minute breaks. Each block pairs a short talk (slides) with the level's exercises. Times assume a class of up to 16 with prerequisites done before day 1.

The last three columns say who uses them:

- **For learners — Workbook:** the level learners work through in the [workbook](../workbook/). Share these links with the class.
- **For the instructor — Slides and Demos:** the deck at the block's first slide, and what you show on the projector from the [instructor demo workbook](demos.html).

Bonus exercises (⭐) are for fast learners during the hands-on blocks; skip them in the schedule. Each level's last bonus is a Playwright test of the level's acceptance criteria (🧪): point fast learners there first, since a suite that passes is also the quickest way to confirm their build before the next level. It needs Node.js 20 or later.

Each level block (Levels 1 to 13) ends with a knowledge check, and its time is included: learners answer the three questions while their last prompt runs, then you spend about 5 minutes going over the answers, from the end of the level's page in the [demo workbook](demos.html).

**Day 2 is tight.** Level 7 has five exercises and five demos. If the class falls behind, in this order:
1. Present D2.3 (AI Creator) and D2.4 (auto-tagging) from their slides, without the live demos (saves about 8 minutes).
2. Let learners who are behind skip 7.4 (the FAQ page collections): nothing later depends on it.
3. Go over only the knowledge check question the room split on most.
